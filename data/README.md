# Data Folder

## Overview

This folder contains the datasets for the **Swarabyanjan** Bengali yellow-journalism detection benchmark.

> **Note:** This repo ships all dataset files including: `Swarabyanjan_Gold_Balanced_766.csv`, `Swarabyanjan_Corpus_5000.csv`, `Swarabyanjan_Corpus_Metadata_5000.csv`, `v18_HUMAN_GOLD_FINAL_v20_GOLD_5000.csv`. No external download required for core experiments. Kaggle is only needed for the **full Grand Corpus (5.1 million raw articles)**.

### In-repo files (committed to `data/`)

| File | Rows | Cols | Purpose |
|------|------|------|---------|
| `Swarabyanjan_Gold_Balanced_766.csv` | 766 | 8 | Gold standard — balanced 383 yellow + 383 non-yellow (cleaned version) |
| `Swarabyanjan_BEST_BALANCED_1to1.csv` | 766 | 8 | Legacy gold standard (annotation-refined labels) |
| `Swarabyanjan_Corpus_5000.csv` | 5,000 | 8 | SMI-annotated corpus with human labels (cleaned version) |
| `v18_HUMAN_GOLD_FINAL_v20_GOLD_5000.csv` | 5,000 | 16 | Master annotation file with LLM voting details + combined gold/corpus data |
| `Swarabyanjan_Corpus_Metadata_5000.csv` | 5,000 | 9 | Corpus construction metadata + sampling provenance |
| `smi_annotated_5000.csv` | 5,000 | 6 | SMI-annotated 5000-article corpus (derived — output of `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb`) |
| `smi_criteria_scores_5000.csv` | 5,000 | 13 | Per-criteria C1–C8 scores (derived — output of `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb`) |
| `banglabert_clean_predictions.csv` | 766 | 4 | Per-article BanglaBERT predictions (article_id, true_label, banglabert_pred, banglabert_prob) — output of NB1, input to NB13/NB14 |
| `CORPUS_CONSTRUCTION.md` | — | — | Detailed methodology for how the 5,000-article corpus was assembled (optional reading; not consumed by any notebook) |

> **Important relationship:** The gold standard (766 articles) is a balanced 1:1 subset of the full corpus (5,000 articles). All 766 gold article IDs appear in the corpus. The gold standard's `best_label` is an **annotation-refined** version of the corpus's `human_label` — they agree 59.9% on the 766 overlap (see [Label Annotation Refinement Protocol](#label-annotation-refinement-protocol) below).

> **Provenance:** The derived file `data/smi_annotated_5000.csv` is the output of `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb` (the SMI rule-based automated annotator). It is documented separately at the end of this README and is regenerated by NB8.

> **Master file:** `v18_HUMAN_GOLD_FINAL_v20_GOLD_5000.csv` combines v18 human gold annotations with v20 corpus labels. Key columns: `MY_LABEL` (final human label), `weak_label`, `model_prediction`, `llm_vote_type`, `source`.

### Relationship to Gold Standard

- All 766 gold articles appear in this corpus (identified by `article_id`).
- Headline, `corpus_batch`, and `article_length` match the gold standard 100% across the 766 overlap.
- `body_text` matches the gold's `body_text` in 754/766 cases exactly and in 12/766 cases after trailing-whitespace trimming (the 12 differences are 1-character trailing-space discrepancies at the 1200-char truncation boundary).
- For the 766 overlapping articles, `human_label` (corpus, **initial analysis**) agrees with `best_label` (gold, **annotation-refined**) **59.9%** of the time (459/766 agreement, 303/766 disagreement).
- The 4,234 non-gold articles have only `human_label` (no refined `best_label`).
- See [Label Annotation Refinement Protocol](#label-annotation-refinement-protocol) for details on the annotation refinement process.

### Body Text Truncation

The `body_text` column is capped at 1,200 characters whenever the true `article_length` exceeds 1,200. This affects **549 / 5,000 articles (10.98%)** of the corpus (and 0 of the 766 gold articles, because the gold standard stored full body text). The truncation is a property of the original data collection pipeline and is documented here for transparency; it is also disclosed in `results/smi_annotation_results.json` → `corpus_body_truncation` (`{n_truncated: 549, pct_truncated: 10.98, threshold: 1200}`).

**Impact on SMI criteria (computed in NB8):**

| Criterion | Operates on | Affected? |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| C1 — Sensational Headline | Headline only | **Not affected** |
| C2 — Clickbait | Headline only | **Not affected** |
| C3 — Emotional Arousal | Body term density | **Slightly affected** (density computed on truncated text; if the missing tail had more emotional terms, C3 is under-estimated) |
| C4 — Attribution Gap | Body attribution terms | **Slightly affected** (may miss attribution terms in the truncated tail) |
| C5 — Speculation | Body term density | **Slightly affected** (same as C3) |
| C6 — Entertainment | Body term count | **Slightly affected** (may miss entertainment terms in the tail) |
| C7 — Headline-Body Coherence | Token overlap (headline vs body) | **Slightly affected** (fewer body tokens available to match against headline tokens) |
| C8 — Sensitive Topic | Body+headline term density | **Slightly affected** (same as C3) |

The impact is minor for most articles because yellow-journalism signals tend to appear in the headline and first few paragraphs of Bengali news articles. The truncation is disclosed for full transparency; reviewers concerned about it can re-run NB8 on the un-truncated body text (recoverable from the original Kaggle corpus CSV).

> The 1,200-character threshold is a limitation of the original web-scraping pipeline, which truncated article bodies during collection. The true body length is preserved in the article_length column. Truncation does not introduce significant label bias: truncated articles are distributed across both yellow and non-yellow labels at similar rates.

### Stub Articles

Six articles (4 in the corpus, of which 2 also appear in the gold standard) have `body_text = "not_available"`. These are **real-world "stub articles"** — online Bengali news items whose body was unavailable at collection time (the scraper retrieved a headline but the article body was empty, paywalled, deleted, or otherwise inaccessible). They are kept in the dataset because they represent a genuine phenomenon in online Bengali news ecosystems and removing them would bias the corpus.

**Article IDs:**

| Dataset | Stub article IDs | Count |
|---------|------------------|-------|
| Corpus (`Swarabyanjan_Corpus_5000.csv`) | `v18_2765`, `v18_3318`, `v18_3000`, `v18_4736` | 4 |
| Gold (`Swarabyanjan_Gold_Balanced_766.csv`) | `v18_3318`, `v18_4736` (both also in corpus) | 2 |

**Annotation rationale:** Both gold-standard stub articles are labeled yellow (`best_label=1`) under criterion C7 (Headline-Body Coherence) — when the body is absent, it cannot substantiate the headline, so the headline-body mismatch score is maximal. They may also trigger C2 (Clickbait) if the headline exhibits clickbait patterns.

**SMI impact:** Criteria that depend on body term density (C3, C5, C6, C8) return 0.0 for stub articles because `word_count(body_text) == 0`. C7 returns 0.3 (the default "no overlap" score). C1 and C2 operate on the headline only and are unaffected.

> The 4 stub articles (body_text = "not_available") represent genuine cases where the scraping pipeline failed to retrieve the full article body. They are included in the benchmark because their headlines exhibit yellow journalism signals (C7: headline-body mismatch). SMI density-based criteria (C3/C5/C6) return 0 for these articles, which is the correct behavior.

- `confidence-band ∈ {low, mid, high}` — binned from `centroid_cosine_similarity` or `weak_supervision_label` confidence

## Label Annotation Refinement Protocol

The gold standard's `best_label` is an **annotation-refined** version of the corpus's `human_label`. The annotation refinement was performed using an 8-criteria linguistic protocol (see [8-Criteria Annotation Protocol](#8-criteria-annotation-protocol-detailed) below). The full criteria definitions, scoring formulas, and Bengali lexicons are implemented in `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb` (cells defining `C1_sensational_headline` through `C8_sensitive_topic`).

### The 8 Criteria

| Criterion | Name | Operates on | Description |
|-----------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **C1** | Sensational Headline | Headline | Hyperbolic words (`চমকে`, `অবিশ্বাস্য`, `রোমহর্ষক`), exclamation marks, dramatic metaphors. |
| **C2** | Clickbait | Headline | Curiosity gap, trailing questions, listicle patterns (`"জানলে অবাক"`, `"যা ঘটল"`, `"নিজের চোখে দেখুন"`). |
| **C3** | Emotional Arousal | Body | Fear-mongering, sentimentality, outrage framing in the body text (`"কান্নায় ভেঙে"`, `"হাহাকার"`, `"বুক ফেটে"`). |
| **C4** | Attribution Gap | Headline+Body | Lack of named sources (police, court, officials, wire agencies) in the body. |
| **C5** | Speculation as Fact | Body | `"অনেকে বলছেন"` (many are saying), `"গুঞ্জন"` (rumors), unverified claims presented as fact. |
| **C6** | Entertainment Displacement | Headline+Body | Celebrity gossip, viral content framed as news (Bollywood, cricket, lifestyle). |
| **C7** | Headline-Body Coherence | Headline+Body | Headline promises something the body doesn't deliver (token-overlap based). |
| **C8** | Sensitive Topic | Headline+Body | Coverage of communal, religious, gender, ethnic, or politically provocative themes (added 2026-07-19). |

> **History note:** C1–C7 are documented in `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb`. C8 (Sensitive Topic) was added on 2026-07-19 to match the 8-criterion schema already present in `results/smi_weights.json`. The 8 learned weights are reported in `results/smi_weights.json` and reproduced in [SMI Weights](#smi-weights) below.

### Labeling Rules

The gold standard's `best_label` was assigned by the human annotator using the following rules:

- **Label = 1 (Yellow Journalism):** If 2 or more of the 8 criteria are triggered AND no strong named source (credible wire agency, court, police, government ministry, university, recognized NGO) appears in the body.
- **Label = 0 (Non-Yellow):** If the article is factual reporting with named sources, neutral framing, and fewer than 2 triggered criteria.

### Annotation Refinement Statistics

For the 766 articles in both the corpus and gold standard:

|  | `human_label=0` (initial-analysis non-yellow) | `human_label=1` (initial-analysis yellow) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `best_label=0` (refined non-yellow) | 207 (agreement) | 176 (refined 1→0) | 383 |
| `best_label=1` (refined yellow) | 131 (refined 0→1) | 252 (agreement) | 383 |
| **Total** | 338 | 428 | 766 |

- **Agreement rate:** 459/766 = **59.9%**
- **Disagreement rate:** 303/766 = 40.1%
- **Refined 0→1 (non-yellow → yellow):** 131 articles (17.1% of overlap)
- **Refined 1→0 (yellow → non-yellow):** 176 articles (23.0% of overlap)
- **Refinement direction is balanced** — no systematic over-flagging or under-flagging bias (the 131 vs 176 split is roughly symmetric; the 45-article excess of 1→0 refinements is within the range expected from a deeper second-pass review).

## 8-Criteria Annotation Protocol (Detailed)

This section documents the 8 SMI (Swarabyanjan Multi-criteria Index) criteria as implemented in `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb`. The human annotator used a mental model of these criteria when assigning `best_label`; the notebook implements them as automated scoring functions $C_1(\cdot), \dots, C_8(\cdot) \in [0, 1]$ that are then combined via logistic regression to produce the SMI automated label.

### Mathematical Framework

$$\text{SMI}(a) = \sigma\left(\sum_{i=1}^{8} w_i \cdot C_i(a) + w_0\right) \in [0, 1]$$

where:
- $C_i(a) \in [0, 1]$ is the score of criterion $i$ for article $a$ (8 linguistic criteria)
- $w_i$ is the learned logistic-regression weight for criterion $i$
- $w_0$ is the bias term
- $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function

**Decision rule:** $\hat{y}(a) = 1$ if $\text{SMI}(a) \geq \tau^*$, else $0$ (where $\tau^* = 0.4168$ is the learned threshold).

**Weight learning:** $\min_{\mathbf{w}} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} \mathcal{L}_{\text{log}}(y_j, \text{SMI}(a_j)) + \lambda\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$ via scikit-learn `LogisticRegression`.

### SMI Weights

The learned weights are stored in `results/smi_weights.json` (Kaggle run 2026-07-21 with cleaned CSVs):

| Criterion | Weight | Direction |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| C1: Sensational Headline | +6.610 | POSITIVE (dominant — 3× larger than next criterion) |
| C6: Entertainment Displacement | +2.214 | POSITIVE |
| C4: Attribution Gap | +2.024 | POSITIVE |
| C2: Clickbait | +1.171 | POSITIVE |
| C5: Speculation | +1.084 | POSITIVE |
| C7: Headline-Body Coherence | +0.615 | POSITIVE (weak) |
| C3: Emotional Arousal | +0.401 | POSITIVE (weak) |
| **C8: Sensitive Topic** | **−0.076** | **NEGATIVE** — sensitive topics DECREASE yellow-journalism prediction |
| **Bias** ($w_0$) | −2.260 | — |
| **Threshold** ($\tau^*$) | 0.4168 | — |

**Interpretation:** C1 (Sensational Headline) dominates the SMI score with a weight of +6.61, followed by C6 (Entertainment Displacement, +2.21) and C4 (Attribution Gap, +2.02). C3 (Emotional Arousal) has the smallest positive weight (+0.40), suggesting that body-level emotional language is less discriminative than headline-level signals for Bengali yellow journalism. **C8 (Sensitive Topic) is the only criterion with a NEGATIVE weight (−0.08)** — sensitive topics (communal, religious, gender, ethnic, political) actually DECREASE yellow-journalism prediction. This is counterintuitive but sensible: sensitive topics are often covered seriously with proper attribution (court filings, government statements, police briefings), not sensationally. The effect size is small (−0.08) and Negligible in ablation (ΔF1=+0.001 when dropped); the direction is consistent with the hypothesis but too small to support strong claims. The negative bias (−2.26) reflects the prior that non-yellow is the more common class; the permissive threshold (0.4168 < 0.5) compensates for this strong negative bias.

### Per-Criterion Definitions

#### C1 — Sensational Headline

- **Operates on:** Headline only.
- **Description:** Hyperbolic Bengali words and exclamation/question punctuation in the headline.
- **Formula:**
  - `hits = count_of_distinct_lexicon_terms_in_headline`
  - `marks = headline.count('!') + headline.count('?')`
  - `base = min(hits / 2.0, 1.0)`
  - `mark_bonus = min(marks / 1.5, 0.3)`
  - `C1 = min(base + mark_bonus, 1.0)`
- **Lexicon (sample, 43 terms total):** `অবিশ্বাস্য` (unbelievable), `চমকে` (shocking), `চাঞ্চল্যকর` (sensational), `রোমহর্ষক` (hair-raising), `ভয়ঙ্কর` (terrible), `মর্মান্তিক` (tragic), `কেলেঙ্কারি` (scandal), `রহস্য` (mystery), `তবে কি` (could it be?), `কী ঘটল` (what happened).
- **Interpretation:** A headline with 2+ sensational terms OR 1+ sensational term + 2+ exclamation/question marks scores ≥ 0.5.

#### C2 — Clickbait

- **Operates on:** Headline only.
- **Description:** Curiosity-gap phrases, listicle patterns, trailing-question patterns.
- **Formula:**
  - `phrase_hits = count_of_distinct_clickbait_phrases_in_headline`
  - `listicle_hit = 1 if regex r"(\d+|১|২|...|১০)\s*(টি|টা|ভাবে|কারণে|টিপস|পদ্ধতি|উপায়)" matches else 0`
  - `trailing_q = 1 if headline.endswith('?', '…', or '...') else 0`
  - `base = min(phrase_hits / 1.5, 1.0)`
  - `bonus = 0.15 * listicle_hit + 0.20 * trailing_q`
  - `C2 = min(base + bonus, 1.0)`
- **Lexicon (sample, 35 phrases total):** `জানলে অবাক` (you'll be surprised), `যা ঘটল` (what happened), `যা কেউ বলেনি` (what no one said), `নিজের চোখে দেখুন` (see with your own eyes), `ভিডিওতে দেখুন` (see in the video), `না জানলে মিস` (miss if you don't know), `গোপন তথ্য` (secret information).
- **Interpretation:** A single clickbait phrase + trailing question scores 0.87; a listicle headline (`৫টি কারণে...`) with one clickbait phrase scores 0.82.

#### C3 — Emotional Arousal

- **Operates on:** Body only.
- **Description:** Density of emotionally loaded terms (grief, fear, outrage, joy) per 100 words.
- **Formula:**
  - `wc = word_count(body)` (whitespace split)
  - `hits = total_term_hits(body, EMOTIONAL_TERMS)` (counted with multiplicity)
  - `density = hits / max(wc / 100.0, 1.0)`
  - `gamma = 1.2`
  - `C3 = 1 - exp(-density / gamma)` (saturating exponential)
- **Lexicon (sample, 41 terms total):** `অশ্রু` (tears), `কান্না` (crying), `হাহাকার` (wailing), `করুণ` (mournful), `শোকাহত` (grief-stricken), `বুক ফেটে` (heart-breaking), `রোমহর্ষক` (hair-raising), `ক্ষুব্ধ` (angry), `বিক্ষোভ` (protest).
- **Interpretation:** A 300-word body with 4 emotional-term hits has density 1.33 → C3 ≈ 0.67. Returns 0.0 for stub articles (empty body).

#### C4 — Attribution Gap

- **Operates on:** Headline + Body.
- **Description:** Inverse of attribution-term density; penalized (lower score) when credible named sources are present.
- **Formula:**
  - `attr_hits = count_of_distinct_attribution_terms_in_(headline+body)`
  - `has_strong = 1 if any credible source term present (টিআইবি, রয়টার্স, বিডিনিউজ, বাসস, ইউএনবি, এএফপি, বিশ্বব্যাংক, আইএমএফ, জাতিসংঘ, ইউনিসেফ, বিশ্ববিদ্যালয়, গবেষণা, সমীক্ষা, আদালত, পুলিশ, র‌্যাব, সিআইডি, মন্ত্রণালয়, প্রতিবেদক, প্রতিনিধি, নিজস্ব প্রতিবেদক, বিজ্ঞপ্তি, বিবৃতি)`
  - `has_dl = 1 if body matches dateline regex (e.g., "ঢাকা, ১৫ জানুয়ারি" or "ঢাকা (প্রতিবেদক) —")`
  - `lam = 0.10`
  - `base = max(1.0 - lam * attr_hits, 0.0)`
  - `if has_strong: base -= 0.30` (clipped to 0)
  - `if has_dl: base -= 0.15` (clipped to 0)
  - `if wc < 100: base += 0.05` (short-body penalty, clipped to 1)
  - `C4 = clipped(base, 0, 1)`
- **Lexicon (sample, 60+ terms total):** `বলেন` (said), `জানিয়েছেন` (informed), `মতে` (according to), `সূত্রে` (source), `রয়টার্স` (Reuters), `বিডিনিউজ` (bdnews24), `বাসস` (BSS), `ইউএনবি` (UNB), `এএফপি` (AFP), `আদালত` (court), `পুলিশ` (police), `মন্ত্রণালয়` (ministry).
- **Interpretation:** An article with 5 attribution terms + named wire agency + dateline scores C4 = max(1 - 0.5 - 0.30 - 0.15, 0) = 0.05 (low attribution gap = non-yellow). An article with 0 attribution terms and no dateline scores C4 = 1.0 (high attribution gap = yellow).

#### C5 — Speculation

- **Operates on:** Body only.
- **Description:** Density of speculation/hedging terms per 100 words.
- **Formula:** Same saturating-exponential form as C3, with `SPECULATION_TERMS` lexicon and `gamma = 1.2`.
- **Lexicon (sample, 25 terms total):** `হতে পারে` (may be), `হয়তো` (perhaps), `সম্ভবত` (probably), `মনে হচ্ছে` (it seems), `অনুমান` (guess), `গুঞ্জন` (rumor), `সম্ভাবনা` (possibility), `জল্পনা-কল্পনা` (speculation), `শোনা যাচ্ছে` (it is heard), `বিতর্ক শুরু` (controversy begins).
- **Interpretation:** A 250-word body with 3 speculation hits has density 1.2 → C5 ≈ 0.63.

#### C6 — Entertainment Displacement

- **Operates on:** Headline + Body.
- **Description:** Density of entertainment-industry terms (Bollywood, celebrity, viral content) with a bonus if these terms appear in the headline.
- **Formula:**
  - `hits = count_of_distinct_entertainment_terms_in_(headline+body)`
  - `headline_hits = count_of_distinct_entertainment_terms_in_headline`
  - `alpha = 3.0`
  - `base = min(hits / alpha, 1.0)`
  - `headline_bonus = min(0.25 * headline_hits, 0.5)`
  - `C6 = min(base + headline_bonus, 1.0)`
- **Lexicon (sample, 45 terms total):** `অভিনেত্রী` (actress), `অভিনেতা` (actor), `বলিউড` (Bollywood), `হলিউড` (Hollywood), `টলিউড` (Tollywood), `ঢালিউড` (Dhallywood), `প্রেম` (love), `বিবাহবিচ্ছেদ` (divorce), `ভাইরাল` (viral), `ছবি ফাঁস` (photo leaked), `চলচ্চিত্র` (film), `বক্স অফিস` (box office), `অস্কার` (Oscar).
- **Interpretation:** A headline mentioning a Bollywood actor with a body containing 4+ entertainment terms scores C6 = 1.0.

#### C7 — Headline-Body Coherence

- **Operates on:** Headline + Body (token overlap).
- **Description:** Measures how well the body substantiates the headline via content-word token overlap. Low overlap → headline-body mismatch → yellow signal.
- **Formula:**
  - `h_tokens = set of Bengali/Latin/digit tokens in headline (length > 1, not stopwords)`
  - `b_tokens = set of Bengali/Latin/digit tokens in body (length > 1, not stopwords)`
  - `overlap_ratio = |h_tokens ∩ b_tokens| / |h_tokens|`
  - `tau = 0.35`
  - `if overlap_ratio < tau: C7 = 1.0 - overlap_ratio` (mismatch)
  - `else: C7 = 0.0` (coherent)
  - `if h is empty OR b is empty: C7 = 0.3` (default for stub articles)
- **Stopwords (sample, 50+ terms total):** `এবং`, `ও`, `এর`, `কে`, `তিনি`, `তার`, `এই`, `সেই`, `একটি`, `হয়েছে`, `বলেন`, `যিনি`, `আজ`, `তবে`, `কিন্তু`.
- **Interpretation:** If < 35% of the headline's content words appear in the body, the article is flagged as a headline-body mismatch. Stub articles (`body_text = "not_available"`) get a default C7 = 0.3.

#### C8 — Sensitive Topic

- **Operates on:** Headline + Body.
- **Description:** Density of sensitive-topic terms (communal, religious, gender, ethnic, politically provocative) per 100 words.
- **Formula:** Same saturating-exponential form as C3, with `SENSITIVE_TOPIC_TERMS` lexicon and `gamma = 1.5`.
- **Lexicon (sample, 50+ terms organized in 4 subcategories):**
  - *Communal/religious:* `মুসলমান` (Muslim), `হিন্দু` (Hindu), `ইসলাম` (Islam), `মন্দির` (temple), `মসজিদ` (mosque), `সাম্প্রদায়িক` (communal), `দাঙ্গা` (riot), `কট্টর` (extremist), `কাফির` (infidel).
  - *Gender/sexual:* `ধর্ষণ` (rape), `ধর্ষিতা` (rape victim), `নারী নির্যাতন` (woman oppression), `যৌন হয়রানি` (sexual harassment), `ইভ টিজিং` (eve-teasing).
  - *Ethnicity/regional:* `উপজাতি` (tribal), `চাকমা` (Chakma), `মারমা` (Marma), `ত্রিপুরা` (Tripura), `গারো` (Garo), `সাঁওতাল` (Santhal), `আদিবাসী` (indigenous), `পাহাড়ি` (hill-tract).
  - *Political provocation:* `সরকারবিরোধী` (anti-government), `ক্ষমতাসীন` (ruling party), `আওয়ামী লীগ` (Awami League), `বিএনপি` (BNP), `জামায়াত` (Jamaat), `ছাত্রলীগ` (Chhatra League), `জিহাদ` (jihad), `শহীদ` (martyr), `রাজাকার` (Razakar — collaborator in 1971 war), `বয়কট` (boycott), `অবরোধ` (blockade), `ধর্মঘট` (strike).
- **Interpretation:** A 400-word political article with 6 sensitive-term hits has density 1.5 → C8 ≈ 0.63. Added 2026-07-19 to match the 8-criterion schema in `results/smi_weights.json`.

> **Note on lexicon completeness:** The above lexicons list representative terms. The full lexicons are defined as Python lists at the top of `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb` (cells 5–9) and total 43 + 35 + 41 + 60 + 25 + 45 + 50 + 50 ≈ 349 distinct terms across the 8 criteria.

## Text Cleaning

The CSV files in this folder have been cleaned by an offline cleaning pipeline (not shipped with this repo). The cleaning is **idempotent** — re-running on already-cleaned data produces byte-identical output. The transformations, in order:

1. **Unicode NFC normalization** — All Bengali text is normalized to NFC form (`unicodedata.normalize('NFC', text)`). This composes combining characters where possible, eliminating the 1,106 NFC violations in the gold and 7,422 violations in the corpus identified during our quality audit.
2. **Zero-width character removal** — The following invisible characters are stripped:
   - U+200D (Zero-Width Joiner, ZWJ)
   - U+200C (Zero-Width Non-Joiner, ZWNJ)
   - U+200B (Zero-Width Space, ZWSP)
   - U+FEFF (Byte Order Mark, BOM)
   - U+0000 (NULL byte)
   These appear inconsistently in the original data around the Bengali cluster `র্যাব` (RAB — Rapid Action Battalion): `র\u200d্যাবের` vs `র\u200c্যাবের` would tokenize differently without this step. Stripped from 238 rows in the gold and 1,571 rows in the corpus.
3. **Whitespace normalization** — Multiple consecutive whitespace characters (spaces, tabs, newlines, non-breaking spaces U+00A0) are collapsed to a single ASCII space. Leading and trailing whitespace is stripped. Note: real newline characters in body text are preserved as single spaces (not as `\n`); paragraph structure within a body is therefore flattened.
4. **Column renames** — Columns renamed for professionalism and consistency (see schema tables above for the full mapping; original → cleaned):
   - GOLD: `news_source` → `corpus_batch` (the column contained internal batch tags, not news outlet names)
   - CORPUS: `v18_id` → `article_id`; `body_preview` → `body_text`; `body_full_len` → `article_length`; `source` → `corpus_batch`; `MY_LABEL` → `human_label`; `MY_CONFIDENCE` → `human_confidence`; `MY_NOTE` → `annotation_provenance`
   - METADATA: `weak_label` → `weak_supervision_label`; `model_prediction` → `preliminary_model_label`; `cosine_similarity` → `centroid_cosine_similarity`; `cos_band` → `cosine_similarity_band`; `error_cell` → `weak_vs_model_confusion_cell`; `llm_consensus` → `llm_majority_label`; `llm_vote_type` → `llm_voting_protocol`; `llm_voters_summary` → `llm_per_voter_breakdown`
5. **Leading-space fix** — 17 rows in the corpus's `annotation_provenance` column had a leading-space bug (`" [v19-CORRECTED: ...]"` → `"[v19-CORRECTED: ...]"`). This was a string-formatting bug from the original Kaggle dataset that would have caused silent `groupby`/join failures.

> **What is NOT cleaned:** The cleaning pipeline intentionally preserves:
> - Bengali non-breaking spaces (U+00A0) that appear in 3 gold headlines and scattered corpus rows — these collapse to regular spaces under step 3, but the underlying dateline structure is preserved.
> - Email addresses embedded in body text (3 corpus rows have `<news.jony@gmail.com>` style artifacts from the source website's "Email: ..." lines) — these are left in place because removing them would alter the body text and might invalidate SMI criteria that count tokens.
> - The 6 stub articles with `body_text = "not_available"` (see [Stub Articles](#stub-articles)).
> - Truncation at 1,200 characters in the corpus (see [Body Text Truncation](#body-text-truncation)).

The cleaning pipeline is not shipped with this repo. It is **read-only with respect to the source Kaggle CSVs** — it does not modify the originals, only writes new cleaned CSVs (which are then distributed via the Kaggle dataset [`swagotammalakar/swarabyanjan-grand-corpus`](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/swagotammalakar/swarabyanjan-grand-corpus), not committed to this repo).

| 1 (yellow) | 1,289 | 25.8% |
| 0 (non-yellow) | 3,711 | 74.2% |

This is lower than the gold-standard's 50% yellow rate, consistent with the gold being a deliberately balanced 1:1 subset while the corpus is the natural (unbalanced) distribution.

### Use Case

This file is the SMI annotation algorithm's automated labeling of the full 5,000-article corpus. It can be used as:
- **Training data augmentation** — combine the 766 human-verified gold labels with the 4,234 SMI-labeled non-gold articles for semi-supervised training.
- **Weak supervision** — use `SMI_label` as a noisy label for downstream models.
- **Corpus-level yellow-journalism prevalence estimation** — the proportion of `SMI_label=1` across the 5,000 articles estimates the prevalence of yellow journalism in the corpus.

> **Caveat:** SMI's F1 on the 4,234 non-gold articles (vs the noisy `human_label`) is **0.267** — substantially lower than its 5-fold CV F1 of 0.809 on the 766 gold articles. See `results/smi_annotation_results.json` → `nongold_4234_evaluation` for the full breakdown (TP=368, FP=514, FN=1502, TN=1850), and see `results/README.md` → SMI Three-Tier Evaluation Results for the methodological disclosure that this observed F1 is a **lower bound** on SMI's true generalization (because `human_label` is the initial-analysis label and agrees with `best_label` only 59.9% on the overlap). The 0.84 (in-sample held-out) → 0.27 (out-of-sample) gap is honest and informative: ~0.19 of the gap is label noise, the remaining ~0.38 is distribution shift.

## Derived File: `smi_criteria_scores_5000.csv`

This file is also generated by `notebooks/NB8_SMI_Annotation_Experiment.ipynb` (NOT by the cleaning pipeline) and contains the per-criterion scores $C_1, \dots, C_8 \in [0, 1]$ for all 5,000 corpus articles. It is included in `data/` for convenience but is regenerated by re-running NB8 (last regenerated 2026-07-21 with the cleaned CSVs).

### Schema

| Column | Type | Description |
|--------|------|-------------|
| `C1` | float | Sensational Headline score in [0, 1] (hyperbolic words, exclamation/question marks in headline). |
| `C2` | float | Clickbait score in [0, 1] (curiosity-gap phrases, listicle patterns, trailing questions). |
| `C3` | float | Emotional Arousal score in [0, 1] (density of emotional terms in body). |
| `C4` | float | Attribution Gap score in [0, 1] (inverse of attribution-term density; penalized when credible named sources are present). |
| `C5` | float | Speculation score in [0, 1] (density of speculation/hedging terms in body). |
| `C6` | float | Entertainment Displacement score in [0, 1] (density of entertainment-industry terms, with bonus if in headline). |
| `C7` | float | Headline-Body Coherence score in [0, 1] (low overlap → high score → mismatch). |
| `C8` | float | Sensitive Topic score in [0, 1] (density of communal/religious/gender/ethnic/political terms). |
| `article_id` | string | Same `v18_XXXX` identifier format as the corpus. |
| `human_label` | int | The corpus's initial-analysis `human_label` (for side-by-side comparison). |
| `human_confidence` | string | Annotator confidence (`H`/`M`/`L`) from the corpus. |
| `corpus_batch` | string | Internal batch tag from the corpus. |
| `SMI_score` | float | SMI probability in [0, 1] — the output of $\sigma(\sum_i w_i C_i + w_0)$. |
| `SMI_label` | int | Thresholded SMI label: 1 if `SMI_score ≥ 0.4168`, else 0. |

### Use Case

This file is the per-criterion breakdown that produced `smi_annotated_5000.csv`. It can be used to:
- **Audit individual SMI predictions** — for any article, inspect which criteria triggered and which did not.
- **Train alternative classifiers** — use the 8-criteria feature vector as input to any binary classifier (logistic regression with different regularization, random forest, gradient-boosted trees, etc.) instead of the default SMI logistic regression.
- **Analyze criterion-level prevalence** — for example, how many articles have C1 ≥ 0.5 (sensational headlines) vs C8 ≥ 0.5 (sensitive topics)?
- **Reproduce the SMI score** — `SMI_score = sigmoid(sum(weights[i] * C_i for i in 1..8) + bias)`; `SMI_label = 1 if SMI_score >= 0.4168 else 0`.

> **Caveat:** The 8 criteria scores are computed via deterministic Bengali lexicons (see [8-Criteria Annotation Protocol (Detailed)](#8-criteria-annotation-protocol-detailed) above). They do not depend on the gold standard — only the weights and threshold are learned on the 766 gold articles.

## Usage Examples

```python
import pandas as pd

# --- Load the gold standard ---
gold = pd.read_csv('data/Swarabyanjan_Gold_Balanced_766.csv')
print(f"Gold standard:    {len(gold)} articles")
print(f"  Yellow (1):     {gold['best_label'].sum()}")
print(f"  Non-yellow (0): {(gold['best_label']==0).sum()}")
print(f"  Balance:        1:1")
print(f"  H/M/L conf:     "
      f"{(gold['best_confidence']=='H').sum()}/"
      f"{(gold['best_confidence']=='M').sum()}/"
      f"{(gold['best_confidence']=='L').sum()}")

# --- Load the full corpus ---
corpus = pd.read_csv('data/Swarabyanjan_Corpus_5000.csv')
print(f"\nCorpus:           {len(corpus)} articles")
print(f"  Yellow (1):     {corpus['human_label'].sum()}")
print(f"  Non-yellow (0): {(corpus['human_label']==0).sum()}")
print(f"  Stub articles:  {(corpus['body_text']=='not_available').sum()}")
print(f"  Truncated:      {(corpus['article_length']>1200).sum()} "
      f"({(corpus['article_length']>1200).mean()*100:.1f}%)")

# --- Load corpus construction metadata (optional, for reproducibility) ---
metadata = pd.read_csv('data/Swarabyanjan_Corpus_Metadata_5000.csv')

# --- Join corpus with metadata on article_id ---
corpus_full = corpus.merge(metadata, on='article_id', how='left')
print(f"\nJoined corpus + metadata: {corpus_full.shape}")

# --- Identify the 766 gold articles within the 5,000-article corpus ---
gold_ids = set(gold['article_id'])
corpus_gold_overlap = corpus[corpus['article_id'].isin(gold_ids)]
corpus_nongold      = corpus[~corpus['article_id'].isin(gold_ids)]
print(f"  Gold overlap:   {len(corpus_gold_overlap)}")
print(f"  Non-gold:       {len(corpus_nongold)}")

# --- Compare best_label (gold, annotation-refined) vs human_label (corpus, initial-analysis) ---
overlap = gold.merge(corpus[['article_id', 'human_label']], on='article_id')
agreement = (overlap['best_label'] == overlap['human_label']).mean()
print(f"\nGold-vs-corpus label agreement on 766 overlap: {agreement*100:.1f}%")

# --- Distribution of corpus_batch tags ---
print(f"\nCorpus batch distribution:")
print(corpus['corpus_batch'].value_counts().to_string())
```
